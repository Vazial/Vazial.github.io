How these numbers are produced

Raw logs stay immutable. A normalising layer folds them into events, metrics are derived from those events, and a publication gate decides what may leave. Changing a definition means discarding the intermediate layers and rebuilding everything — incremental updates drift, and a metric that drifts is worse than no metric.

The raw logs have three properties that make naive counting wrong: forks duplicate over a fifth of all records, each role agent writes to a separate file, and role: user is not the same thing as a person speaking. Folding those happens in exactly one place. The gate names what may be published rather than what may not, and refuses to publish at all when it finds an absolute path, a credential, or a retired project name.
